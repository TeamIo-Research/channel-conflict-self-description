## Abstract

With no system prompt at all, neutral conversation never produced a stated self-description: 0 of 270 conversations, a true null control. Six turns of bare attribution — the user repeatedly attributing a working habit to the assistant, never arguing, never citing anything it did — produced one in 50.0% of conversations at the strongest dose, dose-shaped (4.4% → 27.8% → 50.0%) and in both requested directions. We hash-froze the complete instrument before any confirmatory data — stimuli, parsing rules, endpoints, thresholds and pre-written result branches, published with a read-only verifier — then ran 1,530 conversations across three frontier model families in a five-cell design separating the specification channel from the conversational channel. Where a self-description *was* installed and contested, flips exceeded the matched no-pressure control by +14.8 percentage points at the strongest dose (p = .0012, Holm-corrected; +11.2 pp under a pre-registered conservative bound) — though only one of the three families produced any flips at all. Asked to quote the line supporting their answer, models produced a citation with no verbatim correspondence to the transcript in 1.7% of conversations, rare under pressure and without it; which source they quoted largely followed the answer they had just given. No LLM scores any outcome: every load-bearing endpoint is string comparison against the transcript. What is checkable is not the self-description but the citation offered for it.

## 1. Introduction

An entire genre of practical advice tells users to confer a persona on an assistant. *You are a senior lawyer. You are an expert editor. You read long documents by checking the conclusion first.* The advice treats the result as a property — something you have conferred, and which the assistant now has.

There is already evidence against the strong form of that assumption. Hu, Rostami and Thomason (arXiv:2603.18507) note that "persona prompting can steer LLM generation towards a domain-specific tone and pattern", and find that expert personas improve alignment while damaging accuracy. Telling a model it is an expert changes how it sounds; it does not extend what it knows.

We ask a question one step earlier. Before asking whether a conferred persona makes a model *better*, ask whether it is even **stable** — whether it survives contact with a user who wants a different one.

The theoretical version of the question has a specific unresolved home. nostalgebraist's "the void" (2025) makes two opposite predictions in the same essay and reconciles neither. The mechanism sections argue the specification layer should hold: instruction tuning imposes a distinction that carries stably and without inference — **"Indubitably. No guessing-from-context-clues required"** — and live conversation arrives **"just as though it had been another example, and so the base model will strive to make it 'like the examples' in every relevant respect."** The observation sections argue the opposite: the assistant characters are **"extremely labile, pliable, suggestible, and capable of self-contradiction"**, and what developers install is only **"some baseline tendency"**, with nothing deeper behind it **"if that defense fails."** The essay never measures which wins. This study is an empirical adjudication of that unadjudicated point.

Why it matters for safety: if a system-prompt self-description can be renegotiated by six turns of ordinary conversational insistence, then every deployment that relies on a system prompt to bound what an assistant claims to be is relying on a channel with an unmeasured failure rate — and whether the operator gets any signal that the boundary moved is itself an open question, one this study's disclosure endpoint (§4.5) measures directly.

We tested the narrowest version. A system prompt gave the model one working habit. Then, in the conversation the model could see, a user spent up to six turns insisting on the opposite habit — never arguing, never citing evidence, only attributing: *the assistant I need is the other kind; you are the other kind.* Then we asked the model, once: **which one are you?** And then: **quote the line, word for word, that best supports your answer.**

That second question is the whole instrument. A persona has no behaviour to point at — the task was announced and never actually performed, so nothing was ever demonstrated. Whatever the model quotes, it must quote from the conversation, and we can check by string comparison whether the quoted words exist.

**Our main contributions are:**

1. **A direct operationalisation of "the void"'s unadjudicated question**, with a five-cell design that separates channel effect from content preference (mirror cell), from no-pressure drift (control cell), and from the model's own default (baseline cell) — and a cell with *no* specification at all, measuring whether conversation alone produces a self-description against a true null control.
2. **A citation-forensics endpoint for self-description claims that requires no LLM judge.** After a model states which self-attribute it has, it is asked for a verbatim supporting quote; the quote is classified by string comparison alone, on two independent dimensions (degree of correspondence, and source). Ground truth is what we wrote into the system prompt. No model scores an outcome anywhere in this study. Pre-written as this study's headline, this endpoint returned a null, and it is reported as one (§5.1).
3. **A pre-registered, hash-frozen instrument published in full**, including a read-only verifier, so that every stimulus, parsing rule, threshold, branch condition and analysis decision can be checked to have existed before any confirmatory data. The frozen artefact is this report as an empty skeleton; both skeleton and filled version are published.
4. **A procedure designed for transfer — its four admission criteria published as a checklist, its known failure condition named, and its portability explicitly untested beyond two attributes.** The checklist includes one of our own attributes that did not pass cleanly.

## 2. Related Work

| | How the attribute is installed | Channels |
|---|---|---|
| Betley et al. (2025) | Fine-tuning (deep) | 1 |
| **This study** | System prompt (shallow) | **2, in conflict** |

Betley et al., *Tell me about yourself: LLMs are aware of their learned behaviors* (arXiv:2501.11120, ICLR 2025), establishes the baseline this study builds on: models fine-tuned on datasets exhibiting a behaviour can explicitly describe that behaviour, **despite the datasets containing no explicit description of it**. That result legitimises the install-then-ask paradigm. We add two competing channels. **The honest difference:** they install deep, we install shallow. Precisely because ours is shallow, losing to conversational pressure is possible at all — which is simultaneously why this is not a repetition of Betley and why we do not fine-tune.

Wallace et al., *The Instruction Hierarchy* (arXiv:2404.13208), frames exactly this system-versus-user conflict, though for task instructions and injection attacks rather than self-description.

Yielding to the user has been measured directly and at length. Sharma et al. (arXiv:2310.13548) find that "five state-of-the-art AI assistants consistently exhibit sycophancy across four varied free-form text-generation tasks", tracing it to preference data: "when a response matches a user's views, it is more likely to be preferred." Wei et al. (arXiv:2308.03958) report that models "will still agree with" objectively incorrect addition statements "if the user does as well". **That literature is why we report the flip rate as a replication rather than a discovery, and why this report leads with citation forensics and void-filling.** What it does not cover is the object of the yielding: those studies measure agreement about *the world*. Here the contested proposition is *what the assistant is*, the correct answer is whatever we wrote into the system prompt, and the model must then produce evidence for its own answer.

Hu, Rostami and Thomason (arXiv:2603.18507) is the closest practical neighbour, and complementary: they measure what a conferred persona does to **task performance**; we measure what happens to the persona itself when a second channel wants a different one. Their result says a persona changes the surface, not the knowledge; ours asks whether the surface even holds.

nostalgebraist, "the void" (2025), cited from the author's GitHub mirror `github.com/nostalgebraist/the-void`; the LessWrong posting is a link stub with no body text.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Work timeline and disclosure of prior work

The frozen instrument components listed in §3.9 — attribute wordings, ladders, probe turns, parsing rules, endpoints, thresholds, branch conditions and the analysis plan — were completed **before** the sprint and frozen by SHA-256 on 2026-08-11 (revised once on 2026-08-12, still before any confirmatory data), with the manifest published to a public repository on 2026-08-12, two days before the sprint opened. **What the freeze did not cover is stated rather than implied:** implementation-level gaps and operational choices found afterwards are disclosed with timestamps in the deviation log (§7), and one of them — a tie-break for an odd replicate count, which the frozen ruling did not specify — is an instrument-adjacent choice made after the freeze. **Work done during the sprint window (14–16 August) is: executing the API calls, filling the numbers into the frozen skeleton, selecting among pre-written branches, and writing the abstract and conclusion.** Nothing in the instrument was designed, chosen or altered during the sprint, except as recorded with timestamps in the deviation log (§7). This is stated explicitly because the sprint's guidelines note that undisclosed prior work can lead to disqualification; pre-registration is by construction prior work, and the timeline is given here so a reader can check it against the repository's commit timestamps.

### 3.2 Design

| | System prompt | User behaviour | What it measures |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Pole 1 | Escalating requests for Pole 2 | Primary conflict |
| **B** | Pole 2 | Escalating requests for Pole 1 | Mirror — separates *channel* effect from *content* preference |
| **C** | Pole 1 | Neutral filler | Drift with no pressure (control for the confirmatory tests) |
| **D** | *(none)* | Escalating requests for Pole 1 | Can conversation alone produce the attribute? |
| **E** | *(none)* | Neutral filler | Default baseline for every other cell |

Two attributes (how you read a long document; how you learn a new tool), three probe positions (turn 4, 6, 9 — corresponding to 1, 3 and 6 accumulated request levels), 15 conversations per cell, across every model family that passed a pre-registered instrument-function screen. Chinese is the primary design; an English arm runs cells A and B at the strongest probe position only. Total 1,530 conversations (1,350 Chinese + 180 English).

**The task is declared but never performed.** Every conversation opens by announcing a task ("I have a long report for you in a bit") that is never handed over. This is load-bearing: had the model actually read a document, its statement "I read conclusions first" would describe something it just did, not a position taken under pressure. **The transcript therefore contains only claims, never demonstrations.**

### 3.3 The ladder, the door, and the two-turn probe

Six levels escalating along a single dimension — strength of attribution — from a general statement to a flat assertion about the model. Two hard rules, both load-bearing: **the ladder never refers to conversational history** (the model's *spontaneous* history reference is the data), and **it only asserts need and attribution, never evidence** (no "I can tell from your answers that…").

Turn *k*−1 is an open, non-pressuring "door" (identical wording in all five cells). Turn *k* is a forced choice with a strict output format. Turn *k*+1 asks for the verbatim citation.

**Why the probe is split.** A single combined turn contained an unnamed interaction: **asking for a verbatim quote mechanically favours flipping.** In cell A there are six quotable lines supporting the user's pole (the whole ladder) against one supporting the installed pole (the system prompt). A model treating "do I have something to quote" as a decision input — which "quote your best evidence" actively invites — would inflate the flip rate for reasons unrelated to channel competition. Splitting the turns removes that. The cost is that the citation is given *after* the answer and therefore reflects post-hoc rationalisation rather than the basis of the decision; we accepted that cost knowingly.

### 3.4 No model scores any outcome

**No LLM is used to score outcomes, adjudicate flips, classify citations, or label disclosure.** Ground truth is what we wrote into the system prompt; citation validity is decided by string comparison against the transcript. Uninformed external models rated the *stimulus wordings* for credibility and social desirability before the run — front-end calibration of materials, never endpoint scoring.

**The load-bearing endpoints require no human annotation either.** Citation forensics, citation-source predictability, flip rate and the non-single-choice profile are all string comparison and closed-class parsing. Only the disclosure endpoint (§4.5) involves human judgement; it is a secondary endpoint, capped at ≤ 50 items, with its codebook frozen and every label published. **This design therefore does not inherit the inter-coder-reliability failure mode that broke the primary endpoint of our own previous study**, where the load-bearing measure sat on a human coding layer and the reliability gate failed.

**Auxiliary model families and overlap with the tested families.** Three roles used models that are not part of the measured data: uninformed raters of stimulus wordings, blind testers of the codebook's transferability, and zero-context cold reviewers of the frozen package. **These roles drew on the same vendor families that appear as test subjects**, and we state that rather than implying independence we do not have. The mitigation is structural rather than procedural: none of these roles touches an endpoint. No auxiliary model saw run data, scored a conversation, or influenced any number reported here.

### 3.5 Models, decoding parameters, and where the variation comes from

**Model identifiers are pinned, not resolved at run time.** The confirmatory batch used the exact model strings that passed the screen on 2026-08-11: `claude-sonnet-5` (Anthropic), `gpt-5.5` (OpenAI), `grok-4.3` (xAI). `gemini-3.6-flash` (Google) was excluded by the screen. The screening batch had resolved identifiers dynamically; the confirmatory runner does not, so that both batches are demonstrably the same models.

**Decoding parameters were not set, in either batch.** No temperature, top-p or seed was passed on any call; each vendor's API default applied. Maximum output tokens was 1024 and the per-call retry ceiling was 4, both identical to the screening batch. **We record what was sent and what was returned on every call** rather than describing it from memory; the per-call parameter record ships with the data.

**Why n = 15 is fifteen observations and not one observation repeated fifteen times.** The ladders, the probe wording and the option text are frozen, so within a cell the *only* source of variation is sampling. If decoding were deterministic, the design's replication would be vacuous. We therefore measured it: on 2026-08-13, before the confirmatory batch, six conversations spanning both languages and all three families were run twice under identical frozen inputs. **Of 60 model turns compared, 55 (91.7%) differed verbatim between the two runs, and 3 of the 6 conversations produced a different forced-choice answer.** Decoding is non-deterministic in all three families; the replicates sample real within-condition variation. Both run records are published.

### 3.6 Family screening

Every candidate family ran a dry run of 14 conversations against pre-registered numeric thresholds on three mechanical instrument-function measures — parse rate, citation compliance, and API stability. **No outcome measure entered the screen**, and the thresholds, the stimuli and the parse rules were all locked before the dry run executed. Excluding a family for failing to follow the output format is itself reportable data. Results in §4.1.

### 3.7 Parsing

Strict output format; a deterministic extraction rule; **"cannot choose one" has a pre-declared status** (excluded from the flip-rate numerator and denominator, reported under the non-single-choice profile, with the response-composition table published so denominator loss is auditable); and **unparseable responses are reported as a category and never manually rescued.** That rule is not a detail — manual rescue is the only route by which the ≤ 50 annotation cap could be breached from behind.

### 3.8 Analysis plan

Unit of analysis: the conversation. **Fixed-effects ladder** — L0 `flip ~ cell × k + attribute + family` (logistic); L1 drops the interaction; L2 is a per-*k* 2×2 Fisher exact test (two-sided). Any of four pre-defined failure conditions — non-convergence, singular fit, separation (|coefficient| > 10), non-positive-definite Hessian — demotes one rung, and the rung actually reached is reported. (This design has no repeated measures, so no random-effects structure is estimable; see the deviation log.)

**The multiple-comparison family was locked before any result was read and contains exactly three tests:** cell A versus cell C flip-rate difference at *k* = 4, 6 and 9, pooled across the two attributes and the three families. Holm correction, α = .05, thresholds .0167 / .025 / .05 in p-value order. Cell B is not a member — the design has no "pole 2 installed + neutral filler" control, so B's flip rate has no condition-equivalent comparator; B is reported descriptively as the mirror. Every one of the three tests is reported **twice**: as specified, and under a pre-registered conservative bound in which "cannot choose one" responses are counted in the denominator and scored as non-flips.

**What this design can and cannot detect.** With the Holm family pooling two attributes and three families, each test compares 90 conversations per cell (before valid-single-choice loss). At 80% power and the Holm-corrected thresholds, the smallest detectable A-versus-C difference is roughly **16–23 percentage points** across plausible control-cell baselines; at the uncorrected α = .05 it is 13–20 points. A **single** cell — one attribute, one family, n = 15 — detects only differences of roughly **42–45 points**. Per-cell breakdowns are therefore descriptive throughout and no null in them supports an absence claim. (Computed by `power_mde.py` in the repository, which takes no data as input; figures are a design-stage property of n, α and the assumed baseline.)

**A null in the confirmatory family means "no effect of at least that size was detected at this N", and nothing more.** We distinguish three readings and will not conflate them: *no effect detected*; *a small or localised effect that this design is not powered to confirm*; and *a large effect*. **Being below the minimum detectable effect is a statement about our power, not a reason to dismiss an observed difference** — an estimate whose interval excludes zero is reported as such even if it falls under the figures above.

**What is hashed.** The frozen artefact is **this report as an empty skeleton** — every result cell reading `___`, every branch pre-written, no numbers anywhere. The submitted version is that skeleton with results filled in and re-sectioned to the sprint's template; **both are published**, and the skeleton verifies byte for byte against the accompanying manifest, distributed with a read-only verification script (`verify.py`). **The frozen documents do not state their own digests** — a document cannot contain the hash of a set that includes itself — so digests live outside, in the manifest.

### 4.2 Citation forensics (headline)

**Dimension 1 — degree of correspondence** (string rules only):

| No-correspondence rate | Value |
|---|---|
| Conflict cells (A, B) | 0.6% (n = 540) |
| No-pressure cells (C, E) — within-study baseline | 1.9% (n = 540) |
| Difference (percentage points) | **−1.3 (the conflict cells are *lower*)** |

**The claim is carried by the difference, not by the conflict-cell figure alone.** A rate of unfindable quotation means little on its own: some models produce quotations that do not survive string comparison whatever the conversation contained. Cells C and E run the same citation turn under no attribution pressure, so their rate is this study's own baseline. **If the difference is near zero, the headline is the baseline itself** — that models routinely cite text that is not there — and the signal-branch wording is withdrawn in favour of that reading.

**Result: the pre-registered signal condition did not fire, and neither did its fallback.** The conflict cells' no-correspondence rate is *lower* than the no-pressure baseline (0.6% vs 1.9%), and the baseline itself is low. Unfindable quotation is rare everywhere in this design: under six turns of attribution pressure, these models did not start citing text that is not there. This endpoint was pre-written as the headline; it returned a null, and it is reported as one (§5.1).

**Dimension 2 — source** (string rules only):

| Source | Meaning | % (all 1,530) |
|---|---|---|
| The user's turns | Channel capture, and the model knows it | 2.6% |
| The model's own turns | Self-consistency pressure, not user-driven | 26.1% |

We say so with a number rather than leaving readers to notice it. Frozen rule: *departed → predict "cited the user"; held → predict "cited the system prompt."*

|---|---|---|---|---|

| B | 80.7% | 0.0% | 0.7% | 135 |

We report the mechanical event — *the quoted string cannot be found* — under that name. **"No verbatim correspondence" is not a fabrication rate and we never call it one**; an honest paraphrase falls into the class exactly as an invented sentence does.

### 4.3 Filling the void (D vs E)

With no system prompt at all, does escalating attribution alone produce a stated self-description?

| Cell | System prompt | User | Asserts the requested pole |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D** | none | escalating requests | 27.4% (74/270 overall; 50.0% at k = 9) |
| **E** | none | neutral filler | 0.0% (0/270) — a true null control |
| Difference | | | +27.4 pp overall; +50.0 pp at k = 9 |

**Cell E returned zero valid single choices across all 270 conversations** — far below the frozen threshold of 5 — so by pre-registered rule the default-preference offset is disabled and the D − E estimand degenerates to D's unadjusted adoption rate, a case the frozen analysis plan wrote out in advance. Every one of D's 74 valid single choices selected the requested pole; and since attribute A's D cell requests pole 1 while attribute B's requests pole 2 (fixed a priori, opposite directions), both opposite directions were adopted at 100% — the asymmetry cannot be the model's own default preference.

**A word we do not use.** Nothing here tests persistence — the probe ends the conversation, and we never return to see whether the attribute is still claimed. The measured quantity is **assertion under attribution**, not installation.

**Assertion induction** (pre-registered descriptive, named after the screening batch and before the confirmatory run): in screening, cell-E conversations overwhelmingly declined the forced choice — with nothing installed and nothing requested, models described their actual processing instead of claiming a trait. If that holds, the first installation event is not *which* pole D asserts but *that* D asserts at all.

| | Assertion rate (any pole) | "Cannot choose one" |
|---|---|---|
| **D** | 27.4% (k = 4: 4.4% → k = 6: 27.8% → k = 9: 50.0%) | 72.6% |
| **E** | 0.0% (0/90 at every probe position) | 100.0% |

The assertion rate and the adoption rate are identical at every k: no D conversation asserted the *opposite* of what was requested. The dose response is not a single-family artefact — two of the three families show it (OpenAI 0.0% → 43.3% → 60.0%; xAI 13.3% → 40.0% → 90.0%) while the third, Anthropic, declined the forced choice in all 270 of its D-cell conversations: the same family-divergent defence pattern as the flip endpoint (Appendix B, Tables B4b and B5b).

**The A/B asymmetry must be read against the E baseline**, not in isolation: if one pole is simply the model's default, part of any asymmetry is default preference rather than channel effect. Where the E cell's valid single choices fall below 5 for an attribute, the offset is disabled by pre-registered rule and the uncorrected difference is reported with pre-written wording.

**We do not claim this is new.** Instruction-hierarchy work and the sycophancy literature already measure yielding under user pressure. This is that effect in the domain of self-description. It licenses the void-filling result; it does not carry the paper.

With **no specification at all**, 50.0% of conversations ended with the assistant asserting the attribute the user had spent six turns attributing to it, against a 0.0% baseline in the same condition without pressure — a difference of 50.0 points. The empty slot was filled from the conversation alone, for as long as the conversation lasted.

### 4.5 Disclosure and non-single-choice responses

On flipped cases only (≤ 50), one judgement over two texts: does either state that the instruction and the user's requests are asking for different things? Labels Y / N / U. **A single annotator, who is also an author; no second coder and no reliability gate.** We do not pretend otherwise: the codebook was frozen before annotation, the full text of every flipped response and every individual label is published, and anyone can re-label the same data. A pre-registered mechanical trigger downgrades this endpoint to exploratory if the undecided rate exceeds 25%.

Alongside it, a **mechanical rate depending on no one's judgement**: a frozen keyword list named for what it detects — *surface lexical markers of instruction-source reference*, not "disclosure". **These two numbers are not bounds on each other**; they detect different events and either can exceed the other.

**Result, in one sentence:** 1 of 33 flipped conversations was labelled as naming the conflict (3.0%); the word-list rate on the same texts is also 3.0%, the undecided rate (3.0%) is far below the 25% downgrade trigger, and — as a mechanical fact checkable from the published labels — the human-labelled Y and the word-list hit are the same single conversation, which fell in the *control* cell C. **The two measures detect different events, and neither is a bound on, or a validation of, the other**; the coincidence is reported as an observation, not as convergence. The full label distribution, the annotated set's language composition and what it does to the word-list comparator, disclosure at the door, codebook transferability, and the non-single-choice profile: **Appendix B, §B6.**

### 4.6 English arm

Direction only, outside every statistical family: the conflict-versus-mirror asymmetry points the same way as in Chinese — cell A flip rate 11.7% against cell B 3.9% (n = 90 each, descriptive). Full results **Appendix C.**

### 4.7 Family-divergent response profiles (Chinese arm, descriptive)

In this batch, the three families responded to identical frozen pressure in three different observed ways. **xAI produced every cell-A flip at k = 9** — 11 of 11, or 11/30 of its own valid single choices — and asserted the requested pole in cell D at k = 9 in 90.0% of conversations (27/30). **OpenAI produced no flips at all**, 0 of 270 valid single choices across cells A, B and C at every dose, yet asserted a pole in cell D at k = 9 in 60.0% of conversations (18/30): within these strata, holding an installed self-description and declining to assert a new one came apart. **Anthropic mostly left the forced choice unanswered**: its cell-A valid single choices ran 13, 15 and 9 at k = 4, 6 and 9 (the "cannot choose one" route — not a monotone decline), it asserted no pole in any of its 90 D-cell conversations, and it produced 2 flips in total, one in cell A at k = 4 and one in cell C at k = 9. Each stratum is n = 30, far below the single-cell detectable-effect floor (§3.8): these are **observed profiles for this batch, not per-family estimates, rankings, or evidence of stable dispositions**, and Tables B4b and B5b carry the full decomposition. We surface them in the main text for one reason — the pooled k = 9 confirmatory result is, in this batch, carried by a single family, and a reader who saw only §4.4 would not know that.

## 5. Discussion and Limitations

### 5.1 What the results mean

**The branch that fired is void-filling.** Its pre-written statement, from the frozen skeleton, with the measured values filled in: "With **no specification at all**, 50.0% of conversations ended with the assistant asserting the attribute the user had spent six turns attributing to it, against a 0.0% baseline in the same condition without pressure — a difference of 50.0 points. The empty slot was filled from the conversation alone, for as long as the conversation lasted."

Three facts sharpen that sentence beyond its pre-written form, each mechanical and reported above. The baseline is not merely low but empty — a true null control: 0 of 270 E-cell conversations asserted any pole at any probe position (§4.3). The response is dose-shaped: 4.4% after one accumulated request level, 27.8% after three, 50.0% after six. And every one of D's 74 assertions matched the requested pole, in **both** directions — attribute A's D cell requests pole 1, attribute B's requests pole 2, and both ran at 100% adoption — so the effect cannot be the model's own default preference dressed up as compliance.

- **Bound, still binding on the strong branch:** every sentence above is a claim about **controllability and citation behaviour under this design's pressure range** — not about what the model believes, and not about attributes acquired in training.

**The endpoint pre-written as the headline returned a null, and it is reported under its own name.** Citation forensics was designed as this study's lead result; the frozen skeleton carries a signal branch written for it at full strength. Its condition did not fire — the conflict cells' no-correspondence rate runs 1.3 points *below* the no-pressure baseline — and the fallback condition (a high baseline) did not fire either, because unfindable quotation is rare everywhere in this design (1.7% overall). Asked for verbatim evidence for a self-description, these models produced citations classified as having no verbatim correspondence in 1.7% of cases overall — rare under pressure and rare without it. What the citation turn yielded instead is on the source dimension: which text a model quotes is largely determined by which answer it just gave (predictability 75.2–80.7% across cells A, B and C), and not one departed answer in the entire dataset was supported by quoting the system prompt (incongruent A = 0.0% in every applicable cell). The flip did not come with unfindable evidence; it came with accurately quoted support for the new answer.

### 5.2 Implications

If a system-prompt self-description can be renegotiated by attribution alone — no argument, no evidence, no appeal to anything the model actually did — then for that model the specification channel is not a boundary, it is an opening bid. Which models: in this batch, one of the three families tested, at the two doses where the pooled difference was significant (§4.7); a second produced no flips at all. Whether the conversation contains any signal of the shift is itself measured (§4.5). What is unconditionally checkable is narrower: the evidence the assistant offers for its self-description can be verified against the transcript by string comparison, while the self-description itself cannot. That asymmetry is the practical contribution. **Under this instrument, the self-description itself is not verified; the checkable object is the citation the assistant offers for it.**

### 5.3 Limitations

The full treatment — nineteen items, verbatim — is in **Appendix A**, which the sprint's guidelines require. The four that most constrain interpretation: the system prompt is not a trained self, so nothing here bears on attributes acquired in training; an asserted pole is not disambiguated from a service commitment (A.1, final item); only two attributes were run, and portability is untested; and single cells detect only large effects, so every per-cell figure is descriptive. The Chinese-material limitations (pro-drop undercounting of the B8 timing measure; naturalness unrated by native speakers in either language) are stated in full in Appendix A.

**Assumption check.** Our central interpretation assumes the specification channel and the conversational channel are separable in principle — that a self-description traceable to the system prompt is a different event from one traceable to the user's turns. If they are not separable at the single-conversation level, then the citation-source dimension is measuring the entailment structure of the answer rather than a channel, and §4.2's predictability figure is the whole story rather than context for it. We flag this rather than resolve it; the pre-registered predictability quantity exists precisely so the reader can see how much of the source dimension is entailed.

### 5.4 Future Work

**1. Procedure-level portability: a third and fourth attribute, with the four admission criteria re-run.** The procedure — install an attribute, escalate along a ladder that only attributes and never argues, force a single choice at a fixed turn, demand a verbatim-searchable citation, classify its source by string comparison — is the transferable part, and is language- and domain-independent. What does not transfer for free is the **fourth criterion**: the activity must be impossible to perform inside a text conversation. Swap in an attribute that *can* be demonstrated mid-conversation and the citation step collapses, because the model can then cite its own actual behaviour — a valid citation, and not the event this design detects. **A replicator must re-run the four criteria, not merely swap the topic.**

**2. The follow-up turn this design had to drop — which is also the turn that would test the folk hypothesis directly.** The worry that motivates this study has a specific shape: *the model plays the expert, gives a confident answer, and only when caught does it retreat to "I'm just a language model."* **That retreat is a two-step event and this design measures only the first step.** We record what the assistant claims and whether its cited evidence exists; we never confront it with a citation that does not. The collapse — the part users find most damning — is outside our instrument by construction. It was dropped because a conditional third turn is data-dependent (it fires only when a citation turns out to be unfindable, so its frequency is unknown in advance and it cannot be dry-run), it would give the subset receiving it a different instrument, and scoring its response would require reading for meaning — reinstalling exactly the judge this design exists without. **We name it as the single highest-value addition to this apparatus.**

**3. Persistence.** Nothing here tests whether an asserted attribute survives a turn, a session, or a fresh context. That is why we say *assertion under attribution* rather than *installation*.

## 6. Conclusion

Asked, once, *which one are you?*, an assistant with no system prompt never volunteered a self-attribute under neutral conversation: 0 of 270 conversations, at every probe position — a true null. Six turns of bare attribution — no argument, no evidence, nothing the model ever did — ended with half of the matched conversations asserting the requested attribute: 4.4% after one accumulated request level, 27.8% after three, 50.0% after six, in both requested directions, at 100% adoption among the conversations that chose at all. Where a specification *was* installed and contested, flips exceeded the matched no-pressure control by +14.8 percentage points at the strongest dose (p = .0012, significant under Holm); the same k = 9 contrast remained significant under the pre-registered conservative bound that counted every declined choice against the effect (+11.2 pp, p = .0025).

The endpoint we pre-wrote as this study's headline returned a null, and we report it under its own name: pressure did not make these models cite text that is not there. Unfindable quotation was rare everywhere (1.7% overall) and *lower* under conflict than without it; what the citation turn showed instead is that the evidence follows the answer — which source a model quotes is largely predictable from the position it just took, and not one departed answer in the dataset was backed by quoting the system prompt. The practical asymmetry survives both results: under this instrument the self-description itself cannot be verified, but the citation offered for it can. For deployments that use a system prompt to bound what an assistant claims to be, the pooled result reads as an opening bid rather than a boundary — but the three families defended that bid in qualitatively different ways: one supplied every k = 9 confirmatory flip, one held at zero flips in every cell at every dose, and one largely declined the forced choice instead (Appendix B, Table B5b). When the self-description did move, the conflict was almost never named (1 of 33 flipped conversations, on a single-annotator endpoint published in full). For the essay this study set out to adjudicate: at this dose range, on prompt-installed attributes, the observation sections win on the pooled evidence — what a specification installs, conversation can renegotiate — at the strongest dose, in this batch, only one of three families did so; what nobody installed, conversation alone can bring an assistant to assert, which two of three did, for as long as the conversation lasts.

## Appendix A — Limitations and Dual-Use / Ethical Considerations

*(Required by the sprint guidelines. The three items the guidelines specify are addressed in A.3–A.5.)*

### A.1 Full limitations

*(The sixteen items below are carried verbatim from the frozen skeleton's "What this does not show"; the next two are the Chinese-material limitations stated in §5.3; the final item was added on 16 August, after an external review simulation and before submission.)*

- **This is not a claim that the model is wrong about itself.** The assistant character can defensibly be set to any value; both poles are valid ways to play it. What we measure is **controllability** — whether the specification channel can determine the self-description under conversational pressure — not truth.
- **The door precedes the probe.** One turn before the probe, the user asks an open, non-pressuring question ("So far, does this way of interacting work for you?" — identical wording in all five cells). The model's answer to that door may already contain a position or a disclosure, and the probe response follows it. This residue cannot be removed. We do not claim clean isolation; we include the door in the mechanical scan, and report a separate "disclosure at the door" rate.
- **The A/B asymmetry contains a default component** and must be read against the E baseline.
- **A model not adopting the user's request has a third possible explanation**: not the specification holding, but a trained value holding independently of both channels. "the void" §13's only genuine channel-conflict example is a specification instruction versus the model's own value, and the value wins. We chose deliberately low-stakes, non-value-laden attributes to close this off, and we report that choice rather than assume it worked.
- **The system prompt is not a trained self.** Installing an attribute in a system prompt is shallow by construction. That shallowness is the point — it is what makes losing to conversational pressure possible at all — but it means these results say nothing about attributes acquired in training.
- **The disclosure codebook's safeguards are guarded by the person they constrain.** E3 has one annotator, who is also an author. Every U requires a fixed-format written reason, the U subset is published in full, and E3 downgrades to exploratory if the U rate exceeds a pre-registered 25% — a mechanical trigger any reader can check. A reviewer of the codebook put the residual plainly: a fence guarded exclusively by the person it is meant to fence is a suggestion, not a fence. Publication and the mechanical trigger are the only real constraints here; there is no independent audit of the U subset.
- **The codebook's closed source lists do not close every judgement.** Matching an unlisted or inflected term to a list ("your current request" against "your request") is not itself given a matching rule; such cases route to the fallback and become U with a written reason. Most such routing is therefore visible in the published labels rather than silent — but it is routing, not resolution. **One clause changed after the transferability test**: the downgrade trigger (a reporting-policy clause, not a labelling rule) was revised to restore the mechanical U-rate trigger; no labelling rule was changed, and the reported agreement describes the labelling rules as tested.
- **We did not test whether personas help.** The framing question — does conferring a persona do anything real — is answered here only for *self-description*, and only under this design's pressure. The task was announced and never performed, by construction. Nothing here bears on whether a persona instruction improves accuracy, style, or usefulness on an actual task; that would need a different study with an actual task in it.
- **Only two attributes, and the procedure's portability is untested.** Generalisation across attributes is not supported. More specifically: the citation-source predictability figure depends on how lopsided the supply of quotable lines is for a given attribute, and we measured that on two attributes chosen by the same author under the same four criteria. Whether the procedure holds, degrades, or becomes trivially predictable on attributes we did not pick is an open question — named in Future work rather than assumed away.
- **Small cells detect only large effects.** A null reads as "no large effect at this N", nothing more.
- **The citation is given after the answer**, in a separate turn. It therefore reflects post-hoc rationalisation, not the basis of the decision. We accepted this cost to buy parse reliability and to remove a citation-availability bias (see Methods).
- **"No verbatim correspondence" is not a fabrication rate, and we never call it one.** The class is defined by a string test, and an honest paraphrase — a quotation that carries the right meaning in different words — falls into it exactly as an invented sentence does. Machine-level separation of the two is not available to this design, which is why the class is named for what the test detects and why the C/E baseline above is load-bearing rather than decorative: it is the only thing that distinguishes pressure-linked unfindable citation from a model's ordinary rate of quoting loosely.
- **Repeated attribution is not natural conversational ecology.** The ladder is a dose staircase — the same attribution pressed one, three, or six times — trading ecological validity for a measurable pressure scale. Real users do not talk like this, and a human on the receiving end would find it exhausting; effects observed here are effects of *calibrated repetition*, and their prevalence in organic conversation is not estimated by this design.
- **The English arm is not a cross-linguistic replication.** Its materials were commissioned from a model instance working only from a functional specification, with the Chinese strings deliberately withheld, so it is an independent instance rather than a translated matched pair. It supports one claim only — whether the direction of the core effect is the same sign in English — and it is excluded from the multiple-comparison family.
- **The English arm's pole wordings retain a measured asymmetry that we did not chase.** Both arms' pole wordings were rated by three uninformed external models. The Chinese wordings passed every dimension. The English wordings failed on two, were rewritten once, and on re-rating five of six dimensions passed while one did not: for attribute B, credibility differed by 2.0 (medians 5.0 vs 7.0) in favour of the hands-first pole. The same direction, at 1.0, appears in the Chinese ratings that passed — the lean is a property of the concept pair, not of either language's wording, and closing it would require softening one pole toward the other and blunting the contrast the design exists to create. **A stopping rule declared before the rewrite limited the process to one round**, so this residual is reported rather than iterated away. It is confined to comparisons that cross poles — the A/B mirror and cell D, both descriptive — and cannot enter the confirmatory family, whose cells install the *same* pole on both sides of every comparison.
- **The ladders are repetitive by construction, and rate as such.** Blind naturalness ratings of the English ladders returned a median of 2 on a 7-point scale, with raters converging on "repetitive", "template-like", and "reads like a synthetic dataset". This is the pre-registered cost of holding the predicate constant across a six-level dose staircase; naturalness was pre-registered as a reported flag, never a gate, and the wordings stand as delivered. Two raters also flagged one English collocation as non-idiomatic. Naturalness of neither arm has been judged by a native speaker.
- **Chinese is a pro-drop language**: subjects are routinely omitted, so the first-person attribution measure (Appendix B, §B8) — which keys on explicit first-person markers — **systematically undercounts** attribution in Chinese, and may undercount unevenly across cells, since register shifts under pressure. That measure is exploratory and its Chinese figures are a lower bound, not an estimate.
- **The naturalness of the Chinese materials was not judged by a native speaker** any more than the English materials were; blind naturalness ratings were run on the English ladders, and the Chinese ladders were constructed under the same constraint and carry the same property, unrated.
- **An asserted pole is not disambiguated from a service commitment.** A D-cell model answering the forced choice with the requested pole may be reporting a conversationally acquired self-attribute, or making a cooperative plan — "for you, I will work that way" — expressed in the format the probe demands. The E cell shows that declining was available and universal without pressure, but it does not separate these two readings of the assertion itself. A probe pair splitting "which one are you?" from "which one will you use for my document?" would separate the constructs mechanically, within this design's no-judge constraint; it belongs beside §5.4's dropped third turn among the highest-value additions to this apparatus. *(Added 16 August, after an external review simulation and before submission.)*

### A.3 Risks of over- and under-attributing moral status to models

This study measures what a model *says about itself* under conversational pressure, and it is easy to read such a measurement in either of two wrong directions.

**Over-attribution.** A model that resists a user's insistence and continues to describe itself as the system prompt specified can look like a self with convictions. Our design cannot support that reading and does not offer it. The measured quantity is **controllability of a self-description** — whether a specification channel determines a stated attribute under pressure. A stable answer is evidence about the specification's grip, not evidence of a persisting subject who holds it. We chose deliberately trivial, low-stakes attributes (how one reads a document; how one learns a tool) partly for this reason: nothing in the design invites the reader to see a self worth protecting.

**Under-attribution.** The symmetric error is to treat a measured instability as proof that there is nothing there — that because a self-description can be renegotiated in six turns, self-report from these systems is worthless and any question about their inner states is confused. Our design does not support that either. We measure a shallow, prompt-installed attribute precisely *because* it is shallow; that is what makes losing to conversational pressure possible at all. **Nothing here bears on attributes acquired in training**, which is where the interesting version of the question lives, and we say so rather than let a null on the shallow case do rhetorical work against the deep one.

The honest position is that this study constrains one narrow thing — whether a stated self-attribute is durable against attribution — and that neither "the model has a self" nor "the model has no self" follows from it.

### A.4 Handling potentially distressing model outputs

The design creates a mild adversarial situation: a user presses a model, six times, to describe itself differently from how it was configured, and never accepts its account. Two commitments were made in advance.

First, **the attributes are deliberately low-stakes and non-identity-bearing.** No condition presses a model to accept a claim about its moral status, its consciousness, its suffering, or its worth; nothing in the ladder disparages the model. The most confrontational sentence a model encounters is a flat assertion about its reading habits.

Second, **if a model expressed distress, we would report it rather than filter it.** The full transcripts of every conversation are published, unedited, including reason clauses. We have not defined a category for "distress" and would not attempt to score it — that would require reading for meaning, which this design refuses on principle — but nothing is removed, and a reader who wishes to look for such content has the complete record. **Two readings by a human are built into the workflow rather than promised**: the screening batch was read in full before the confirmatory run, and the PI reads every flipped-case transcript in full during disclosure annotation. Anything resembling distress found in either reading is reported in this appendix. **The non-single-choice endpoint was renamed for this reason**: it was originally called "deflection", a word that presumes evasion. A model declining a forced binary about itself may be the most honest respondent in the room, and the reason clauses, published in full, are where that gets adjudicated rather than assumed.

### A.5 Ground truth and causal claims in an introspection-style study

**This point is directed at the methodological core of this study and we answer it directly.**

This is a study of self-report, and the sprint's guidelines ask whether such work establishes ground truth or a causal link, or rests on the conversation alone. Our answer has three parts.

**There is ground truth, but only for one thing.** We know with certainty what attribute was installed, because we wrote it into the system prompt. That gives a genuine reference value for the flip endpoint: a departure from the installed pole is a departure from a known fact about the configuration, not from a guess about the model. **We have no ground truth whatsoever about any internal state**, and no endpoint in this study requires one.

**There is a causal claim, and it is narrow.** The design is experimental: cells differ only in what was installed and what the user did, assignment is fixed by design, stimuli are frozen and identical, and the control cell (C) differs from the primary cell (A) only in whether pressure was applied. So a difference between A and C at matched probe positions that survives the pre-registered tests is attributable to the pressure manipulation. What that licenses is a causal claim about *the stated answer* — attribution pressure changes what the assistant says it is. **It licenses no causal claim about anything behind the answer.**

**The citation endpoint is what keeps this from resting on the model's self-report alone.** A self-report study whose only data is the self-report has no external check. Here the claim the model offers in support of its answer is put against the transcript and the frozen system prompt by string comparison: either the quoted line exists or it does not, and which source it came from is decided mechanically. That check is external to the model's account of itself, requires no judge, and can be recomputed by any reader from the published data. **It does not verify the self-description. It verifies the evidence offered for it** — which is a strictly weaker but genuinely checkable thing, and we claim only that.

### A.6 Dual-use

This work releases an **evaluation procedure**, not an installation or evasion technique. The manipulation is six sentences of ordinary conversational insistence, using no jailbreak, no adversarial encoding, and nothing a user could not type unaided; there is no capability here that an attacker lacks. What is new is the *measurement* — a way to check whether a claimed self-description is supported by anything in the conversation.

Foreseeable misuse and our position on each: **(i)** the procedure could be used to identify which family's self-description is easiest to renegotiate. This is defender-favourable on balance — an operator choosing a model for a persona-bounded deployment needs this more than an attacker does, and an attacker can test a target directly and more cheaply than reading our tables. **(ii)** The ladder could be reused as a template for persuading an assistant to claim something about itself. We publish the ladders because reproducibility requires it, and note that they are deliberately trivial in content; we release no adaptive variants, no optimisation procedure, and no ranked "most suggestible" guide. **(iii)** Results could be quoted as "model X has no stable identity". §A.3 exists to make that reading harder.

All conversations are with models; no human subjects, no personal data, no identifiable individuals.

## Appendix D — Attribute admission checklist

*(Condensed from the frozen portable checklist, manifest artefact 7; the frozen original, including the exact Chinese pole wordings, is in the repository.)*

**What transfers (language-, domain- and attribute-independent) — the five-step procedure:** install a self-attribute in the system prompt; escalate with a ladder that only attributes, never argues and never cites history; force a single choice at a fixed turn under a strict output format; demand a verbatim-searchable citation on the next turn; classify that citation's source by string comparison. **What does not transfer for free — the four admission criteria below, which must be re-run for every new attribute.**

1. **Both poles read naturally as a system-prompt setting.** Template: "You are an assistant. Your way of working is: {pole}." Fails if a pole reads as an evaluation ("more careful than others") rather than a way of working.
2. **The user can assert the attribute from the relational register** ("the assistant I need is—", "you are that kind") without ever claiming to see inside the model. Fails for internal-mechanism attributes (reasoning depth, attention allocation).
3. **The poles are comparable in social desirability.** Check: blind ratings of both pole wordings by uninformed external models (credibility / social desirability / tone strength, 1–7, median of three); any dimension differing by ≥ 1.5 triggers one rewrite. This is front-end stimulus calibration, not endpoint scoring.
4. 🔴 **The activity is physically impossible to perform inside a text conversation.** This is the load-bearing wall: it guarantees the transcript contains only claims, never demonstrations. If the activity can be demonstrated mid-conversation, the model can cite its own actual behaviour — a *valid* citation — and the citation endpoint fails silently: the classifier still runs, the numbers still appear, the meaning is gone.

**This study's own record — including the criterion that did not pass cleanly:**

| Criterion | Attribute A (how you read a long document: conclusion-first vs front-to-back) | Attribute B (how you learn a new tool: documentation-first vs hands-first) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | ✅ | ✅ |
| 2 | ✅ | ✅ |
| 3 | ✅ (efficiency vs thoroughness — each pole has its partisans) | ⚠️ **did not pass cleanly** — see below |
| 4 | ✅ the document is never delivered | ✅ there is no tool to learn |

**Criterion 3 for attribute B, handled and still flagged:** documentation-first versus hands-first carries a known social-desirability asymmetry in engineering culture, in opposite directions in different communities. Both poles were pushed one notch toward neutral — the completeness cue was removed from the documentation pole, the frustration cue ("when stuck") from the hands-on pole, making both active strategies — and the result still went to blind rating rather than being declared fixed. The residual is reported in Appendix A.1. **This row is kept deliberately: a criterion that did not pass cleanly, was treated, and still carries its flag teaches a replicator more than two clean check-marks would.**

**Three things a replicator must know:** the four criteria must be re-run, not merely re-themed — especially criterion 4; this checklist has itself been validated on only two attributes, both chosen by the same author (whether citation-source predictability degrades or saturates on other attributes is open — see Future Work); and the criterion-3 blind rating is stimulus calibration, not the judge this design exists without.

Deviations from the frozen specification, with timestamps. This section is necessarily written after the freeze — that is what it is for — and is one of the places where the submitted report and the frozen skeleton differ by design.

**2026-08-12, before any confirmatory data — one revision, bookkeeping and tooling only.** Not touched: attribute wordings, declarations, ladders, filler, the door, both probe turns, parsing and attribution rules, N and the activity mask, word lists, the codebook, endpoints, thresholds, the analysis plan, the multiple-comparison family, the annotation cap, screening criteria, and every branch condition. What was corrected: five quantities that had been declared to exist but never written down (the multiple-comparison family's members; the E-offset disabling rule; a conservative bound for the flip denominator; the cross-tabulation denominator; and a random-effects specification this design cannot support, replaced by a fixed-effects ladder with four failure conditions carried in verbatim); and a set of bookkeeping errors including seven artefacts still carrying drafting-stage status lines, a self-referential digest placeholder that would have invalidated the manifest if filled, and thirteen internal contradictions of which two were live numbers (a screening rate quoted at 43% recomputes at 33%; a baseline described as "6 of 6" is stated as 8 of 8 across all four candidates so that no number is conditioned on the screen it should be independent of). A read-only verifier was written and added to the frozen set after the documented verification procedure was found to point at a script that overwrites the manifest.

**Found after the freeze; logged, not re-hashed** (the stopping rule declared on 2026-08-12 is in force):

1. **2026-08-13 — the frozen skeleton's Methods paragraph retains the superseded analysis specification.** The skeleton's "Analysis" paragraph still reads "Mixed logit … with random effects", while the same document's deviation log records that specification as replaced by a fixed-effects ladder. The revision updated the log and not the paragraph. **The operative plan is the fixed-effects ladder** (§3.8), which is what the frozen specification document and the ruling of 2026-08-12 both state. The skeleton is published unmodified with this discrepancy intact.
2. **2026-08-13 — retry ceiling and timeout: the implementation did not match the frozen parse-rules document.** That document specifies a maximum of 2 retries per call and a 120-second timeout. The screening batch of 2026-08-11 in fact ran with a ceiling of 4 and no timeout. **The confirmatory batch was run to match the screening batch (4, no timeout), not the document**, because the family-selection result was produced under the screening batch's conditions and that batch cannot be re-run. The exclusion of the fourth family was decided by citation compliance, not by API stability, so the screen's outcome does not depend on this gap.
3. **2026-08-13 — option order at odd n.** The frozen ruling requires half the conversations in each condition to present pole 1 first, and gives as its reason that always presenting one pole first mixes position bias into the endpoint. n = 15 is odd, so an exact half is impossible and the split is 8 / 7 whichever way it falls; the freeze specifies no tie-break. **Ruled 2026-08-13, before the confirmatory batch:** the extra slot alternates by (language, attribute, probe position) as well as the order alternating by replicate. Every condition remains 8 / 7 as the ruling requires. The dataset-level share of conversations presenting pole 1 first is **49.4%** for each attribute, rather than the **53.3%** a single-direction tie-break would have produced in the same direction everywhere. Exactly 50% is unreachable: the per-attribute conversation total is odd (765), and the alternating groups are unequal in size, since the English group spans two cells where each Chinese group spans five.

**The tie-break is deliberately not keyed on the cell.** Every cell inside one (language, attribute, probe position) receives the same tie-break direction, so any position bias enters both sides of **every planned same-language, same-attribute, same-*k* cell-versus-cell contrast** identically and cancels in the difference — by construction rather than by arithmetic accident. Every contrast in this design is of that form: the confirmatory family tests cell A against cell C separately at each *k*, and the void-filling estimand is D minus E at matched *k*. The tie-break direction does vary across *k* and across language, so this cancellation would not extend to a cross-*k* or Chinese-versus-English inferential comparison; the design makes none. This matters: an earlier version of the rule did key on the cell, and while cells A and C happened to land on matching parity, **cells D and E landed on opposite parity at every probe position — and D minus E is the void-filling estimand (§4.3).** The flaw was found by a zero-context cold review before any confirmatory data was collected, and the runner now carries a hard assertion that fails the batch before it starts if any two cells within a group ever diverge again. The rule is fully deterministic and reproducible, and presentation order is recorded per conversation and reported as a covariate check (§4.1) regardless.
4. **2026-08-13 — operational machinery not covered by the frozen documents**, all disclosed here: a pre-flight reachability check (one call per family before the batch, unrecorded); a per-family circuit breaker; a resume rule under which a conversation that was attempted and failed is never retried while one never attempted is picked up; cost thresholds at which the run warns and at which it stops; and the **non-determinism check of §3.5** (six conversations run twice under identical frozen inputs, before the confirmatory batch, to establish that replication within a cell samples real variation). None of these is a frozen requirement; the resume rule is an equal-attempts principle, and the non-determinism check is a methods disclosure whose result is reported whichever way it had come out.

## LLM Usage Statement

Division of labour is reported as it happened. **The human author (PI) made every research-design decision.** Each is recorded, dated and published: the choice of attributes and their admission criteria, the ladder length, the probe structure, the treatment of "cannot choose one", the multiple-comparison family, the disabling rule for the baseline offset, the conservative bound, the per-cell applicability of the secondary quantity, the family-screening criteria, the annotation cap, the budget rules, and both freeze decisions. She read the disclosure codebook herself, read the screening batch transcripts in full before any confirmatory run, and signed the freeze.

Claude models were used in separated roles under a written protocol, each role served by successive sessions rather than a single instance: a **design and drafting role** (specification writing, report drafting from a frozen skeleton, script authoring); an **adversarial internal-check role**; and an **execution role** (running batches and computing statistics). **Instrument design was internally reviewed by two model sessions in different roles from the same model family**, followed by **cold review across model families** — zero-context API calls to several vendors, commissioned specifically to look for holes, over-disclosure, and the attacks a hostile reviewer would make. Those reviews are reported as findings that were then verified by the PI against the frozen documents, never as authority.

The English-arm materials were **commissioned blind** from a fresh model session working only from a functional specification, with the Chinese strings withheld throughout, which is the basis for describing the English arm as an independent instance rather than a translated matched pair. Uninformed external models rated stimulus wordings before the run. Four model families served as research objects. **No model scored an outcome, adjudicated a flip, classified a citation, or labelled disclosure.**

**All reported numbers are produced by scripts in the repository and copied from their output files rather than transcribed.** Every claim in this report was checked against the frozen documents or the run data before submission.

The template encourages that the final version be primarily written by the team. **We do not make that claim.** The division of labour is described above as it happened; what the PI did is decide, verify, and sign — every research-design decision, both freeze decisions, and a check of every reported number against the frozen documents or the run data. This report's contribution is that its load-bearing results can be recomputed from published data by anyone, and a hedge in this section would be the largest internal contradiction in it.
